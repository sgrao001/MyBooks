# Failure

I am a stranger in my birth land. I am reminded of this, and often. 

Dad immigrated to this land. He rapidly adjusted to the individualism he was offered. Individualism, that gave him free expression, growth and prosperity. 

I was required to make do without that which others of my age took for granted - a sense of belonging, a community, a people. Individualism has no value to a child. It is scary, demeaning and isolating. I looked and felt different. My meager childhood attempts at conformance were no match for a judgmental world. I grew up assigning imagined inadequacies in me, as the cause for this loss; a child’s accommodation of the acute pain induced by this absence. 

My childhood needed two parts to make me whole. A home and a community. I had one. I had no ability to acquire the other. I found no help in my attempts to do so. My childhood was left incomplete. This mattered to me and no one else. 

How could he be so blind to my need? Why, if he cared, did he not see my sensitivity to this missing part? Why did he not see the struggle, me compensating in so many ways? Why did he not see large chunks of my childhood slipping away in this pursuit? What handicapped him from helping me with my need? Why would he leave it up to me to overcome this pain?  Why would he not offer a helping hand? Why did we not talk? What was so difficult to see - I was safe at home, but stood apart outside? 

I am now past my childhood. However well-meaning they may be, offers of, What can I do now? How can I help?, much like apologies, serve no purpose. Redemption has an expiry date. This pain is mine. Healing will be mine too. I do not owe anyone my acceptance of attempts at “explaining it away”. Explanations are devoid of meaning. Much will be left unsaid, once more.   

Perhaps my footfalls in Bombay will uncover experiences of my dad’s childhood that I can employ to fill some of the glaring gaps in mine. 

I seek my own solace. I will find my own acceptance. I will accept my acceptances. 

Forgiveness may come as a needed step to aid in my journey to healing.
